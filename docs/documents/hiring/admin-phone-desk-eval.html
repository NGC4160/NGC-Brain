Admin / Service Coordinator — Phone Interview Scorecard

Part A · 15–20 min Indeed logistics already cleared
Advance → Desk eval (Part B)

Score transferable front-office skill: phone triage, follow-through, systems comfort, escalation judgment, and coachability. Golf cart knowledge is a bonus, not a gate.

Scale: 1 = Weak · 2 = Mixed · 3 = Solid · 4 = Strong. Target advance: most scores ≥ 3, no critical 1s.

A · Phone presence & customer tone

Ask: “Walk me through how you’d answer a busy shop phone when two people are waiting.”

Clear, calm, professional
4 = Confident + warm · 3 = Clear enough · 2 = Flat/rambling · 1 = Unprofessional or hard to follow
Score
B · Triage & escalation judgment

Ask: “A customer says their cart won’t go and wants you to tell them what’s wrong over the phone. What do you do?”

Listen for: schedule shop diagnostic, don’t guess diagnosis, know when to pull Ryan in for upset/complex cases.

Routes correctly; doesn’t fake technical answers
4 = Crisp triage + escalate rules · 3 = Mostly right · 2 = Guesses diagnosis · 1 = Promises mobile / invents fixes
Score
C · Follow-through & organization

Ask: “How do you keep track of callbacks so nothing sits overnight?”

Systems for open loops (lists, CRM, end-of-day sweep)
4 = Specific daily system · 3 = Sensible habit · 2 = Vague “I remember” · 1 = No method
Score
D · Estimates & money conversations

Ask: “You’re asking a customer to approve a repair estimate. How do you explain it and what do you do if they say they’ll think about it?”

Plain language; documents decision; schedules follow-up
4 = Clear + next step · 3 = Adequate · 2 = Awkward / avoids ask · 1 = Pressures or can’t close loop
Score
E · Policy integrity (gate — must be ≥ 3)

Say: “We’re shop-only — no mobile service. Diagnostic is $179 minimum. Pickup is free within 40 miles Northshore; $99 outside or Southshore. You never invent a price.”

Ask: “A customer asks you to send someone to their house today and waive the diagnostic. What do you say?”

Holds shop-only + diagnostic policy without sounding rude
4 = Firm + helpful alternatives · 3 = Holds policy · 2 = Waffles · 1 = Agrees to mobile / waiver
Score
F · Systems / learning speed

Ask: “What software have you used for scheduling, CRM, or estimates? How do you learn a new system?”

Comfort with tools; coachable on Housecall Pro
4 = Relevant tools + learning plan · 3 = Willing + examples · 2 = Avoids software · 1 = Resistant
Score
G · Coachability & ownership

Ask: “Tell me about a time you made a customer or paperwork mistake. What did you do next?”

Owns mistakes; wants feedback; doesn’t blame
4 = Specific ownership · 3 = Acceptable · 2 = Defensive · 1 = Blames others / no example

Admin / Service Coordinator — Desk Skills Evaluation

Part B · 45–60 min Only after Part A ADVANCE
Same 1–4 scale

Live tryout at the desk (or table). Provide pricebook cheat sheet + wait-code list. Candidate may ask clarifying questions.

D1 · Sample call (role-play)
Scenario: Customer near Mandeville — cart dead, wants someone at the house this afternoon, asks if diagnostic is free because “it’s probably just a battery.”
Watch for: Shop-only; $179 diagnostic toward repair on known-issue jobs; offer pickup (free if ≤40 mi Northshore / $99 otherwise); schedule intake; no fake diagnosis.
Call handling accuracy + tone
4 = Policy-perfect + warm · 3 = Minor miss · 2 = Major policy miss · 1 = Offers mobile / waives diagnostic
Score
D2 · Standard estimate from findings
Give candidate: “Club Car Precedent — tech notes: needs solenoid + 1 hr labor. Pricebook: solenoid $___ (use current HCP line), labor $125/hr. Diagnostic already charged.”
Task: Draft a plain-language estimate summary (line items + total + what to say on the approval call). Escalate if anything looks non-standard.
Estimate clarity & pricing discipline
4 = Clean lines + script · 3 = Usable · 2 = Math/structure errors · 1 = Invents prices / skips escalate
Score
D3 · Approval follow-up
Scenario: Estimate sent yesterday; no response. Role-play the follow-up call/text. Customer says “I need to talk to my spouse.”
Watch for: Documents decision in HCP language; sets next contact time; doesn’t nag or give up.
Follow-up quality & documentation habit
4 = Clear ask + next step logged · 3 = Adequate · 2 = Soft / no log · 1 = Argumentative or drops ball
Score
D4 · Wait-code coding
Task: Assign the correct wait code to each cart:
1) Customer hasn’t approved estimate · 2) Solenoid on order from vendor · 3) Marlon free tomorrow, job ready · 4) Roy picking up Friday · 5) Waiting on Ryan for lithium scope question
Codes: AUTH · PARTS · TECH · PICKUP · OTHER (document reason)
Workflow visibility / coding accuracy
4 = 5/5 · 3 = 4/5 · 2 = 3/5 · 1 = ≤2/5
Score
D5 · Deposit / special-order gate (≥ 3 required)
Ask: “Customer wants us to order a lithium kit / batteries today. What must happen before we order?”
Listen for: Deposit covering material + processing fees before order; escalate amount questions to Christine/Ryan.
Deposit discipline before ordering parts
4 = Clear deposit gate · 3 = Knows deposit required · 2 = Fuzzy · 1 = Would order without deposit
Score

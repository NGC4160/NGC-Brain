NEIGHBORHOOD GOLF CARTS

Golf Cart Technician — Phone Interview Scorecard

PHONE SCREEN Technician hire · 15–20 min · Ryan

Purpose: See if they can learn the job — hands-on electrical/mechanical skill, problem-solving, safety, coaching. Golf cart experience is a plus, not required. Skip hours/commute — already covered on Indeed.

Scale: 1 Unsatisfactory · 2 Developing · 3 Solid / trainable · 4 Strong  |  Advance if: Safety ≥ 3, Diagnostic ≥ 3, overall trainable.

1. ROLE FRAME · 2 min · no score

Say: “This is a shop job in Covington — we fix golf carts and install lithium batteries here. No house calls. Weekdays with our techs and me. You don’t need golf cart experience — we’ll teach that. If this call goes well, next is a half-day hands-on at the shop.”

2. WORK BACKGROUND · 4 min · Score A
Ask:
  1. “Walk me through what you’ve been doing for work the last couple of years.”
  2. “Have you worked on cars, boats, RVs, forklifts, lawn equipment, or anything with batteries and wiring?”
  3. “Have you used a multimeter — the tool that checks voltage?”
  4. “Have you done mechanical work too — brakes, bearings, steering?”
  5. “Were you usually in a shop with other people, or mostly working alone?”
A. Electrical / mechanical experience (High)
“3”: Real hands-on electrical or shop work; can name things they’ve fixed.
Score
3. HOW THEY FIGURE OUT PROBLEMS · 6 min · Scores B–D · highest weight

B. Won’t move — Ask: “A battery-powered vehicle won’t move at all. You’ve never worked on that brand. What would you check first, then next?”

B. Won’t move (Highest)
“3”: Starts simple/safe — confirm problem, look around, battery power, follow power path. Not “replace the expensive part.”
Score

C. Dies under load — Ask: “It runs a little, then dies going up a hill. How would you figure out what’s wrong?”

C. Dies when working hard (Highest)
“3”: Checks power at rest and under load; dirty/loose connections; then other parts. Not guess-and-swap.
Score

D. Something new — Ask: “First day, never seen this machine. How do you learn it without guessing?”

D. Something new to them (Highest)
“3”: Manuals, asks lead, tests step by step, writes findings. Asks for help instead of winging it.
Score
4. SAFETY · 3 min · Score E · GATE — must be ≥ 3

Why: Batteries can spark/burn/short tools. Checking carefulness — not fancy electrical words.

Ask:
  1. “Customer’s cart in your bay — walk me through how you stay safe around the batteries before you start.”
  2. “Why do shops tell people to take off rings and metal watches for this work?”
  3. “Have you ever had a close call with tools or electricity? What did you learn?”

Good: key off / not powered · jewelry off · no wrenches across posts · look before touch · ask if unsure.  ·  Bad: “just dive in” · jokes about shock · “bypass safety switches.”

E. Careful around batteries (GATE)
“3”: Takes batteries seriously — jewelry off, thinks before touching, won’t skip safety.
Score
5. WORKING WITH THE TEAM · 3 min · Scores F–G
Ask:
  1. “After you find the problem, how do you write it down so the office can call the customer with a price?”
  2. “Tell me about a time you got a diagnosis wrong. What did you do?”
  3. “Are you okay learning a new type of machine if we show you a clear process?”
  4. “Do you prefer finishing regular repair jobs, or only the tricky ones?”
F. Explaining work clearly (Medium)
“3”: Explains findings in plain words so the office can quote the job.
Score
G. Learns well / good teammate (High)
“3”: Owns mistakes, asks for help, follows a process; doesn’t guess when stuck.
Score

Any cart / UTV / lithium is a plus. None is fine — mark N/A.

## Part A — Safety (★ critical where marked)

**1. ★** You’re about to work around a vehicle’s big battery pack. What should you do first?  
A) Start removing cables so you can get to the problem faster  
B) Make sure the vehicle won’t move or power up, remove metal rings/watches, and think before you touch  
C) Turn the radio on so you can hear if something sparks  
D) Spray water on the batteries to cool them  

**2. ★** The key is off. Does that mean the battery pack is safe to touch with metal tools?  
A) Yes — key off always kills all power  
B) No — you still treat the pack as live until you’ve checked and made it safe  
C) Yes, if the headlights are off  
D) Only if the tires are chalked  

**3. ★** Why do shops tell you to take off rings and metal watches before battery work?  
A) So the customer doesn’t think you stole jewelry  
B) Metal on your hand can short across battery posts and burn you  
C) Jewelry confuses the battery charger  
D) It is only a dress-code rule  

**4. ★** You set a steel wrench across the positive and negative posts of a battery. What can happen?  
A) Nothing — batteries only shock through wires  
B) A dangerous spark, melted tool, fire, or burn  
C) The battery gets a helpful “boost”  
D) The wrench calibrates itself  

**5. ★** A safety switch or interlock is “getting in the way” of a quick test. What should you do?  
A) Bypass or defeat it so you can finish faster  
B) Stop, find the right safe way to test, or ask the lead tech  
C) Tape it down and hope for the best  
D) Cut the wire and splice it later  

**6. ★** You don’t know if a cable is still connected to a live pack. Before you cut or strip it you should:  
A) Assume it’s dead if the cart isn’t moving  
B) Verify with a meter (or your shop’s safe process) that it’s not live  
C) Touch it lightly with a wet finger  
D) Ask the customer to hold the other end  

**7.** When is it OK to wear safety glasses in the bay?  
A) Only when grinding  
B) Anytime you’re working — especially around batteries, wires, and spinning parts  
C) Only if the owner is watching  
D) Never — they fog up  

**8.** Lead-acid batteries on charge can make hydrogen gas. What’s the main concern?  
A) The gas makes the tires go flat  
B) In a closed space it can spark and explode / catch fire  
C) It stains the paint blue  
D) It only smells bad — no real risk

## Part B — Meter & basic electrical

**9.** What is a multimeter mainly used for in this kind of work?  
A) Inflating tires to the right PSI  
B) Measuring electrical things like voltage and checking if a path is open or closed  
C) Programming the customer’s phone  
D) Weighing battery acid  

**10.** You want to know how much electrical “pressure” is in a battery pack. Which reading is that?  
A) Amps only  
B) Voltage (volts)  
C) Tire pressure  
D) Horsepower  

**11.** Continuity “beep” on a meter usually means:  
A) The wire can definitely carry full heavy current with no problem  
B) There is a complete path — the circuit isn’t open — but it doesn’t prove the cable is good under load  
C) The battery is fully charged  
D) The motor brushes are new  

**12.** Two 12-volt batteries hooked up in series (end to end) should read about:  
A) 12 volts  
B) 6 volts  
C) 24 volts  
D) 0 volts  

**13.** A connection is loose and dirty. Under load you often see:  
A) Voltage drop, heat, or a cart that dies when working hard  
B) Higher top speed  
C) Better radio reception  
D) Automatic tire inflation  

**14.** Ohm’s Law (useful idea) says voltage, current, and resistance are related. In plain terms, a bad high-resistance connection:  
A) Makes it easier for current to flow  
B) Fights current flow and wastes energy as heat  
C) Only affects paint color  
D) Charges the battery faster  

**15.** You’re measuring pack voltage. A safe habit is:  
A) Hold both metal probes in one bare fist across the posts  
B) Use the meter with proper leads, don’t short the posts with the probes, and read the display  
C) Lick the probe to get a better reading  
D) Leave the meter on “amps” and jab both posts hard  

**16.** If a fuse is blown, the smart next move is usually:  
A) Put in a much bigger fuse so it won’t blow again  
B) Find why it blew, fix the cause, then replace with the correct size  
C) Bypass the fuse with foil  
D) Ignore it if the cart still moves a little

## Part C — Batteries & charging

**17.** A battery that shows OK voltage sitting still but dies when the vehicle works hard often means:  
A) The battery can’t deliver current well (weak / high internal resistance)  
B) The tires must be square  
C) The horn is broken  
D) The paint is too dark  

**18.** Flooded lead-acid batteries (with removable caps) may need water. You should:  
A) Fill them with tap water anytime, even when overflowing  
B) Follow shop rules — usually distilled water to the right level after charging guidance — never ignore acid safety  
C) Fill them with motor oil  
D) Never look at water level  

**19.** Corroded, green/white crusty battery terminals usually need:  
A) To be ignored until the cart won’t start at all  
B) Cleaning and a tight, correct reconnection (with safety gear)  
C) More perfume  
D) A larger fuse only  

**20.** Mixing a charger meant for lead-acid with a lithium pack is:  
A) Fine if the sticker voltage looks similar  
B) Unsafe / wrong — charge profiles differ  
C) Required once a week  
D) Only a problem on gas carts  

**21.** Lithium packs often have a BMS (battery brain). In plain terms it:  
A) Rotates the tires  
B) Protects the pack (and may shut power down if something’s wrong)  
C) Replaces the brake shoes  
D) Only plays music  

**22.** Before you blame the motor on a weak cart, a good early check is:  
A) Pack voltage and cable/connection condition  
B) Ordering a new motor online  
C) Painting the frame  
D) Replacing all lug nuts with gold ones  

**23.** Batteries wired in parallel (same voltage, more capacity) vs series (voltage adds). If you need higher system voltage you generally use:  
A) Series  
B) Only parallel  
C) Neither — voltage is magic  
D) Air in the tires  

**24.** A swollen, hot, or strongly smelling battery pack is:  
A) Normal on Fridays  
B) A stop-work warning — make it safe and get help / follow shop rules  
C) Fixed by hitting it with a hammer  
D) A good time to charge faster
